# Research

My research focuses on applied microeconomics, innovation, and public policy, with a particular interest in how gender inclusion shapes the scale and direction of technological change. I work with large administrative and patent datasets, quasi‑experimental methods, and reproducible workflows in Stata and R. Much of my work examines how institutional design, human capital composition, and regional characteristics influence innovation outcomes, especially in sustainability‑oriented technologies.

Empirically, I build region‑year panels from OECD REGPAT microdata, implement name‑based gender assignment following Carpentier & Raffo (2023), classify technologies using CPC codes (including Y02/Y04S for climate‑related patents), and estimate fixed‑effects models that allow for non‑linear relationships. My research combines careful identification strategies with policy‑relevant interpretation and reproducible empirical pipelines.

## Master’s Thesis  
### **The Role of Gender Inclusion in Green Innovation**  
*MSc in Economics · Universitat de Barcelona · 2026*  
**Grade: 8.8/10**

My thesis studies how female participation in inventive activity affects innovation outcomes, with a particular focus on green technologies. Using a region‑year panel built from OECD REGPAT microdata, inventor‑name gender assignment, CPC‑based technological classification, and regional socioeconomic controls, I estimate fixed‑effects models that test for non‑linear effects.

> “The results show a robust inverted‑U pattern between female inventor share and both total and green patenting in absolute terms.”  
> *(Master’s Thesis, Abstract)*

### **Key Contributions**
- Constructed a region‑year panel from 12.6M inventor‑region observations  
- Implemented gender assignment for 87.4% of inventor‑region pairs  
- Classified green patents using CPC Y02/Y04S  
- Estimated region and year fixed‑effects models with quadratic terms  
- Distinguished between **scale** (patent counts) and **direction** (green share)  
- Conducted heterogeneity analysis across GDP, team size, and green intensity  
- Developed a full replication package (to be uploaded once anonymized)

### **Main Findings**
- Female inventor participation increases total and green patent output  
- The relationship follows an **inverted‑U** pattern  
- Gender inclusion affects the **scale** of innovation more than its **direction**  
- Effects are strongest in **low‑GDP regions**, where misallocation is more severe
